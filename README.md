## Overview
Universities depend on digital systems to manage student records, examinations, faculty information, research activities, financial transactions, and campus networks. These systems face cybersecurity risks that can affect data confidentiality, integrity, and availability.
This project examines the major cybersecurity risks associated with university systems and proposes security policies, an incident response plan, and user awareness measures to reduce those risks.
The project was developed as part of a college cybersecurity assignment and focuses on research, risk analysis, and defensive security practices.

## Objectives
- Identify major cybersecurity threats affecting university systems.
- Assess risks according to their likelihood and potential impact.
- Propose security policies to protect university information and infrastructure.
- Outline an incident response plan for managing cybersecurity incidents.
- Recommend awareness measures to promote safe digital practices among students, faculty, and staff.

## Organization Selected

**Organization type:** University  
**Context:** A university environment similar to South Asian University (SAU).

Universities operate multiple interconnected systems, including student information portals, examination platforms, faculty systems, research databases, administrative services, and campus Wi-Fi networks.

The diversity of users and services makes cybersecurity risk management essential for protecting institutional information and maintaining the availability of academic services.

## Major Cybersecurity Risks
The following cybersecurity risks may affect university systems and digital services.

### 1. Examination System Exploitation
- **Threat:** Unauthorized access to examination portals or manipulation of academic records.
- **Potential Impact:** Compromised examination integrity, altered results, and disruption of academic activities.

### 2. Unauthorized Access
- **Threat:** Access to student, faculty, or administrative accounts without permission.
- **Potential Impact:** Account compromise and misuse of confidential information.

### 3. Data Breaches
- **Threat:** Exposure of personal, academic, or financial information.
- **Potential Impact:** Privacy violations, identity theft, and reputational damage.

### 4. Phishing and Social Engineering
- **Threat:** Fraudulent emails, messages, or websites designed to deceive users.
- **Potential Impact:** Stolen credentials, compromised accounts, and unauthorized access.

### 5. Malware and Ransomware
- **Threat:** Malicious software that damages systems, steals data, or encrypts files.
- **Potential Impact:** Data loss, financial costs, and disruption of university services.

### 6. Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS) Attacks
- **Threat:** Flooding online services with excessive traffic.
- **Potential Impact:** University websites, portals, and other digital services may become unavailable.

### 7. Insecure Campus Wi-Fi
- **Threat:** Weak network security or poorly configured wireless access.
- **Potential Impact:** Increased risk of unauthorized access and network-based attacks.

### 8. Outdated Software
- **Threat:** Software containing unpatched, known vulnerabilities.
- **Potential Impact:** Attackers may exploit these weaknesses to compromise systems.

### 9. Insider Threats
- **Threat:** Misuse of legitimate access by students, employees, contractors, or other authorized users.
- **Potential Impact:** Unauthorized disclosure, modification, or deletion of information.


## Risk Assessment Approach

The risks are assessed qualitatively using two factors:

- **Likelihood:** How likely a threat is to occur.
- **Impact:** How seriously the threat could affect university operations, information, or services.

These factors help categorize risks into priority levels so that appropriate security measures can be recommended.

The assessment is intended as an academic analysis, not as a verified audit of any university's internal infrastructure.


## Proposed Security Policies
The project recommends the following policies:
1. **Password Security:** Use strong, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication for sensitive accounts.
2. **Access Control:** Grant permissions according to user roles and the principle of least privilege.
3. **Network Security:** Protect campus networks using firewalls, secure Wi-Fi configurations, monitoring, and regular security reviews.
4. **Data Protection and Backup:** Protect sensitive information through appropriate access controls, encryption, secure backups, and recovery testing.
5. **User Awareness:** Train students and staff to recognize phishing, follow safe browsing practices, and report suspicious activity.


## Incident Response Plan
The proposed incident response process follows five stages:
1. **Identification:** Detect and assess suspicious activity or a potential security incident.
2. **Containment:** Limit the incident's spread and reduce further damage.
3. **Eradication:** Remove the threat and address the vulnerabilities that enabled it.
4. **Recovery:** Restore affected systems and services securely.
5. **Review:** Document lessons learned and improve security measures.

The plan supports the fundamental principles of the CIA Triad:

- **Confidentiality:** Prevent unauthorized access to sensitive information.
- **Integrity:** Protect information from unauthorized modification.
- **Availability:** Maintain access to essential university systems and services.

Authentication is also an important security control for verifying user identities.


## Cybersecurity Tools and Learning
Tools such as Metasploit Framework, Nmap, and Wireshark are relevant to understanding vulnerability assessment, network scanning, and network traffic analysis.
Studying these tools can help explain how security professionals investigate weaknesses, examine network behavior, and support defensive security decisions.
Any practical testing should be restricted to systems for which explicit authorization has been granted.


## User Awareness Measures
The project recommends:
- Conducting regular cybersecurity awareness sessions.
- Teaching users to recognize phishing emails and suspicious links.
- Encouraging strong passwords and multi-factor authentication.
- Avoiding untrusted downloads and unsafe websites.
- Establishing a clear process for reporting suspicious activity.


## Key Takeaways
- Cybersecurity risk assessment helps organizations prioritize threats.
- Effective security policies reduce the likelihood and impact of cyber incidents.
- Incident response planning supports timely detection, containment, and recovery.
- User awareness is an important part of an institution's overall security strategy.
- Protecting university systems requires a combination of technical controls, clear policies, and responsible user behavior.

## Scope and Limitations
This is a theory-based academic project. Its risk ratings and recommendations are intended for educational analysis and are not the result of a formal security audit of an actual university.

The proposed policies and incident response procedures provide a foundation for understanding university cybersecurity risk management. They do not replace institution-specific assessments or professional security testing.

## Disclaimer
This project is intended for educational and defensive cybersecurity purposes. It does not claim to have compromised, tested, or identified vulnerabilities in any university's internal systems.

All security testing discussed in the project must be conducted only on authorized systems and within the approved scope.
